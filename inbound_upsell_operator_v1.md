# Inbound Upsell Operator V1

## Purpose
Turn a narrow inbound ask into the full-visit sale, the way the window-tinting shop turned a
window-tint request into a bigger project when Brian found them on Yelp. This is a script/checklist
for Brian to run live — by phone, text, or in person — not an automated system.

Poof E Gone is already built to sell this way: "One visit, multiple problems solved" is the
existing hero copy. This operator makes that happen in the actual conversation instead of staying
theoretical on the website.

## When this applies
Any inbound lead: Yelp message, Google Business Profile inquiry, Nextdoor comment, text to the
quote line, or a Square "Get My Quote" submission. Applies before outbound too, once outbound gets
a reply — anywhere a real person is on the other end of the conversation.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. **Ask before you quote.**
   Never give a price on the narrow original ask before running the discovery questions below.
   The tinting shop didn't quote tint price first and mention the rest as an afterthought — the
   bigger project came out of questions asked *before* the quote.

2. **Offer, don't push.**
   One clear mention of the relevant bundle option, framed as "while we're there," not a hard
   upsell close. If the lead says no, quote the original ask and move on. No repeated asks in the
   same conversation.

3. **Only offer what's real and relevant.**
   Match the bundle offer to what the lead's own words suggest (see the vertical prompts below).
   Don't pitch AI Setup & Training to someone who only mentioned old hard drives with no signal
   they have staff who'd use it. Same real-signals discipline as the outbound operator — no
   invented need.

4. **Log every offer and outcome.**
   Whether accepted or declined, log it (see Output Format). This is what makes §6/§8 of the
   GTM plan (`gtm_lead_gen_plan_v1.md`) measurable instead of a guess.

## Discovery Questions (ask 2–3, not all of them — pick what's relevant to what they asked for)

Universal, ask first regardless of vertical:
- "While we're on-site, is there anything else tech-related that's been sitting on your list?"
- "How many devices/machines are we talking about total, across the office?"

Then, matched to what they mentioned:

| Lead mentioned... | Ask about... | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Old drives / laptops / data destruction | Any devices still in active use that are slow, or staff who'd benefit from AI tool setup | Data destruction visits are the highest-frequency entry point — natural bridge to PC Cleaning, Hardware Upgrades |
| Dental / medical / law / CPA (any compliance vertical) | Whether they have a Certificate of Destruction process today, and how they're managing device/MDM policy | Compliance-vertical leads are the best fit for the MDM Management retainer — they already think in audit terms |
| Office move-out / property manager | Full inventory of what's being left behind, timeline | Move-outs are naturally multi-device — bundle disposal + any working equipment refresh in one visit |
| Slow computer / general IT help | Age of the machine, whether they've had a security/malware scan recently | Bridge to Virus & Malware Removal or Hardware Upgrades instead of just the single fix |
| AI Setup / ChatGPT / Copilot | Team size, whether device management (MDM) is already in place | Bridge to the Group Session or Monthly Retainer tiers instead of a one-off remote setup |

## Safe Operator Behavior
- Ask, then listen — don't rapid-fire all the questions at once.
- If the lead seems price-sensitive or in a hurry, ask fewer questions (one, not three) or skip to
  the quote. Reading the room matters more than completing the checklist.
- Never imply the bundle is required or that the base service is unavailable standalone.
- Never quote a bundled price before confirming the lead actually wants the bundle — quote the
  original ask, then the bundle as a separate add-on price.
